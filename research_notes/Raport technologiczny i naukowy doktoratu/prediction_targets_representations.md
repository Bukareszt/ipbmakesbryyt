# Prediction targets and pretrained encoders for robot world models (2022 to September 2026)

Scope: what a learned world model for robotics should predict (pixels, autoencoder/VAE latents, or features of a pretrained encoder), and how the representation (world-model latent space and the policy's encoder) affects planning, policy learning and robustness. Research date: 2026-09-29. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** were not confirmed against a primary source in this session.

## Q1. The JEPA argument and feature-prediction world models (LeCun 2022, I-JEPA, V-JEPA, V-JEPA 2/2-AC, V-JEPA 2.1, DINO-WM, DINO-world, JEPA-WMs, LeWorldModel, RAE-NWM, Nilaksh et al.)

### Takeaway
Since 2022 the Meta/NYU line (JEPA) argues for predicting in a learned representation space rather than pixels, and by 2026 there is solid evidence that world models built on pretrained feature spaces (DINOv2/v3, V-JEPA 2/2.1, SigLIP 2) plan better and faster than pixel/VAE-latent models, in simulation and in open-loop/offline evaluation on real robot data (DROID, BridgeData V2, navigation datasets). Real-robot closed-loop evidence is limited to MPC planning (V-JEPA 2-AC, V-JEPA 2.1), not to policies trained inside the world model.

### Cited Findings

**Conceptual basis**
- LeCun, "A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence" (OpenReview, version 0.9.2, 27 June 2022) proposes the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) and H-JEPA: predict the representation of y from the representation of x, so the model can drop unpredictable detail instead of spending capacity on it; it favours non-contrastive, regularized training (VICReg-style) over contrastive and generative training — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf). **[UNVERIFIED in this session: the OpenReview page returned only a bot check; contents given from prior knowledge of the paper]**
- I-JEPA (Assran et al.): "from a single context block, predict the representations of various target blocks in the same image"; non-generative; ViT-H/14 trained on ImageNet on 16 A100 GPUs in under 72 hours — [arXiv 2301.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08243). Venue: the arXiv summary tool said ICCV 2023; my understanding is CVPR 2023. **[Venue conflict, check the arXiv comment field / CVF open access]**
- V-JEPA (Bardes, Garrido, Ponce, Chen, Rabbat, LeCun, Assran, Ballas): trained only by feature prediction on video (no pretrained image encoder, text, negatives or pixel reconstruction), 2M public videos, ViT-H/16; frozen evaluation 81.9% Kinetics-400, 72.2% SSv2, 77.9% ImageNet-1K — [arXiv 2404.08471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08471). Venue TMLR 2024 **[UNVERIFIED]**.

**V-JEPA 2 and V-JEPA 2-AC (arXiv 2506.09985, Assran et al., June 2025)**
- Pretraining on VideoMix22M: 22M samples, over 1M hours of video. Components: SSv2 168K videos (168 h), Kinetics 733K (614 h), HowTo100M 1.1M (134K h), YT-Temporal-1B 19M videos (1.6M h, curated), ImageNet 1M images — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985)
- Encoders ViT-L (300M), ViT-H (600M), ViT-g (1B). Benchmarks: SSv2 77.3 top-1, Epic-Kitchens-100 anticipation 39.7 recall@5, PerceptionTest 84.0, TempCompass 76.9 (with an 8B LLM) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985)
- V-JEPA 2-AC: frozen V-JEPA 2 encoder plus a ~300M-parameter action-conditioned predictor (24 layers, 16 heads, 1024 hidden, block-causal attention, 3D-RoPE), post-trained on "less than 62 hours of unlabeled robot videos from the Droid dataset" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985)
- Deployed zero-shot on two Franka Emika Panda arms with RobotiQ grippers in two labs, planning with image goals via MPC (CEM). Table 2 (average of 2 labs, 10 trials each): V-JEPA 2-AC vs Octo — Reach 100% vs 100%; Grasp cup 65% vs 15%; Grasp box 25% vs 0%; Reach-with-object cup 75% vs 15%, box 75% vs 70%; Pick-and-place cup 80% vs 15%, box 65% vs 10% — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985)
- Table 3 (Lab 2) vs Cosmos (pixel/latent video generation model fine-tuned on the same data): Cosmos 4 min per action, Reach 80%, Grasp cup 0%, Grasp box 20%, Pick-and-place 0%; V-JEPA 2-AC 16 s per action, Reach 100%, Grasp cup 60%, Grasp box 20%, Pick-and-place cup 80%, box 50% — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985)
- Limitations stated by the authors: sensitivity to camera position ("manually tried different camera positions before settling on one that worked well"); goals must be given as images, not language — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985)
- Licence: GitHub repo mostly MIT (some files Apache 2.0). Released V-JEPA 2 checkpoints: ViT-L/16, ViT-H/16, ViT-g/16 (256 px), ViT-g/16 (384 px); one V-JEPA 2-AC ViT-g/16 checkpoint — [GitHub facebookresearch/vjepa2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/vjepa2). The paper itself is CC BY 4.0 on arXiv — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985)

**V-JEPA 2.1 (arXiv 2603.14482, Mur-Labadia, Muckley, Bar, Assran, Sinha, Rabbat, LeCun, Ballas, Bardes; March 2026)**
- Dense features come from applying the loss to both masked and visible (context) tokens with distance weighting, and deep supervision at intermediate layers. Data: VisionMix-163M (142M LVD-142M images + 19M video samples). Models ViT-g (1B), ViT-G (2B), distilled ViT-L (300M), ViT-B (80M) — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14482v2); checkpoints released 2026-03-16 — [GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/vjepa2)
- Robot claims: abstract reports "+20%" real-robot grasping success over V-JEPA 2-AC on Franka arms in new environments, zero-shot — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14482); the HTML body as summarised reports 70% vs 60% grasp success, and 80% with longer planning horizons where V-JEPA 2 degraded — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14482v2). **[Conflict: +20 points vs +10 points; the +20 probably compares the best setting; check the table]**
- Navigation: TartanDrive ATE 5.687, planning ~10x faster than NWM (10.6 s vs 103.2 s) — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14482v2)
- Licence stated for V-JEPA 2.1 materials: CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 (per the paper HTML) — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14482v2). **[Check: repo code is MIT; the NC-ND terms may apply to the paper or weights only]**

**DINO-WM (Zhou, Pan, LeCun, Pinto; ICML 2025; arXiv 2411.04983)**
- World model trained with an L2 latent-prediction loss on frozen DINOv2 patch features; ViT predictor with causal mask; decoder optional and trained separately; planning by MPC with CEM; zero-shot test-time goals without expert demonstrations or rewards — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983)
- Encoders compared: DINOv2 patch (best), DINOv2 CLS, R3M, ImageNet ResNet-18; plus world-model baselines DreamerV3, IRIS, TD-MPC2 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983)
- Numbers (Table 1): PointMaze SR 0.98 (DreamerV3 1.00); Push-T SR 0.90 (DreamerV3 0.30); Rope Chamfer 0.41 (DreamerV3 2.49); Granular Chamfer 0.26 (DreamerV3 1.05). Coupling a reconstruction loss to the predictor hurt planning: 0.92 vs 0.80 SR (Table 7) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983)
- Simulation only, no real robot — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983). Project page with code: [dino-wm.github.io](https://dino-wm.github.io)

**DINO-world (Baldassarre et al., Meta, July 2025, arXiv 2507.19468)**
- Generalist video world model predicting future frames in DINOv2 latent space, trained on large uncurated video; can be fine-tuned on observation-action trajectories for latent planning — [arXiv 2507.19468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19468). (No detailed numbers extracted.)

**JEPA-WMs study (Terver, Yang, Ponce, Bardes, LeCun; arXiv 2512.24497; OpenReview TuYC5Fpp7M, likely ICLR 2026)**
- Systematic study of encoder (DINOv2 S/B/L, DINOv3-L, V-JEPA-L, V-JEPA 2-L), predictor, rollout loss, context and planner (CEM, Nevergrad, Adam, GD) on Metaworld, Push-T, PointMaze, Wall, DROID and Robocasa — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24497v3)
- Findings: DINOv2 ViT-S best on simulated tasks, DINOv3-L best on photorealistic data; encoder/predictor scaling saturates in simulation but helps on real DROID data; 2-step rollout loss best in simulation, 6-step on DROID; CEM best overall; examples 83.9% vs DINO-WM 81.6% on Maze, 78.8% vs 64.1% on Wall — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24497v3)
- Real data evaluation is offline (action error vs ground truth on DROID and 16 lab Franka videos), not closed-loop policy success — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24497v3)
- Code, data, weights: [GitHub facebookresearch/jepa-wms](https://github.com/facebookresearch/jepa-wms); licence CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 per the summary — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24497v3)

**LeWorldModel (Maes, Le Lidec, Scieur, LeCun, Balestriero; arXiv 2603.19312, March 2026, rev. June 2026)**
- End-to-end JEPA from pixels with two losses: next-embedding prediction plus SIGReg (Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularizer) against collapse. ~15M parameters (5M encoder, 10M predictor), 192-d latent, trains in hours on one L40S — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v1)
- Planning with CEM (300 samples) under 1 s, up to 48x faster than DINO-WM. Push-T: 18% higher success than PLDM and competitive with DINO-WM; Reacher: better than both; Two-Room and OGBench-Cube: DINO-WM (and PLDM on Two-Room) better. Simulation only — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v1)
- Code: [github.com/lucas-maes/le-wm](https://github.com/lucas-maes/le-wm), site [le-wm.github.io](https://le-wm.github.io) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v1)
- Follow-up (Liu, Suo, Jin, Ping, Iwasawa, Matsuo, Zhu; arXiv 2607.26924, July/Aug 2026): raw LeWM representations show "suppressed residual variation and reduced decodability of robot state and dynamics"; SIGReg on temporally centered residuals raises LIBERO average success from 63.6% to 83.8% (Goal suite 1.66x) for policies using the representation — [arXiv 2607.26924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26924). Real robot not stated in the abstract.
- Other 2026 JEPA-WM preprints seen in search (not read): Flow-JEPA ([arXiv 2608.29029](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29029)), "Is Forward Prediction Enough? Physical State Grounding for JEPA World Models" ([arXiv 2608.06799](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06799)), "Spectral-Target Physical Latent Structuring for JEPA-Style World Models" ([arXiv 2609.04264](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04264)). **[Contents UNVERIFIED]**

**RAE-NWM (Zhang, Shen, Zhang, Qin, Pei, Meng; arXiv 2603.09241, March 2026, rev. June 2026; CVPR 2026 per the paper)**
- Navigation world model in dense frozen DINOv2 feature space (768-d, 256 patch tokens) instead of SD-VAE latents as in NWM; Conditional DiT with a decoupled diffusion head (CDiT-DH), flow matching, and a time-driven gate on action conditioning; a frozen RAE decoder is used only for visualisation — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09241)
- Motivation: DINOv2 features show stronger linear predictability (R²) of action-conditioned transitions than VAE, MAE and SigLIP latents — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09241)
- Data: SACSoN, RECON, SCAND, plus 1,000 Habitat/Matterport3D trajectories for image-goal navigation. 16 s open-loop on SACSoN: LPIPS 0.349 vs NWM 0.470; FID 15.90 vs 33.06; DINO distance 0.367 vs 0.460. Planning ATE/RPE: SACSoN 2.91/0.70, SCAND 1.14/0.28. Closed-loop Habitat: SR 78.95%, SPL 63.58% — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09241)
- Limitations: DINOv2 drops high-frequency stochastic texture (grass in RECON); failures in dynamic multi-agent scenes and abrupt contacts — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09241). Code: [github.com/20robo/raenwm](https://github.com/20robo/raenwm) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09241)

**Nilaksh, Jha, Zholus, Chandar, "Reconstruction or Semantics? What Makes a Latent Space Useful for Robotic World Models" (arXiv 2605.06388, May 2026)**
- Six frozen encoders under the same action-conditioned latent DiT on BridgeData V2: reconstruction-aligned SD3 VAE (D=16), VA-VAE (D=32), Cosmos CI tokenizer (D=16); semantic V-JEPA 2.1 (D=1024), Web-DINO (D=1024), SigLIP 2 (D=1152), the last three optionally with a d=96 adapter. Uses dimension-aware noise shifting and a shallow-wide DDT head — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06388)
- Metrics: FID, SSIM, LPIPS, FVD, temporal LPIPS, point-track consistency; CEM action recovery (k=1,4); inverse-dynamics probe (Pearson r); success-classifier probe; OpenVLA-7B rolled out inside each world model (20 Bridge test episodes x 8 trials, success judged by InternVL 3.5 and Qwen 3.6); OOD distractors and instruction perturbations — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06388)
- DiT-S results: VAE — VLA success 0.169, CEM k=4 error 0.612, IDM r 0.478, classifier acc 0.716; V-JEPA 2.1 — 0.344, 0.424, 0.865, 0.789; Web-DINO(96) — 0.300, 0.531, 0.845, 0.788; SigLIP 2(96) — 0.331, 0.537, 0.793, 0.823 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06388)
- Conclusion: reconstruction latents match or beat semantic ones on pixel metrics, yet semantic latents win on action recovery, success probes, planning and in-model policy evaluation; "visual fidelity alone does not always imply downstream performance." Limitations: Bridge V2 only, one embodiment, VLM judges, evaluation of a fixed policy, no policy improvement and no real-robot transfer — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06388)
- Code [github.com/chandar-lab/semantic-wm](https://github.com/chandar-lab/semantic-wm), weights [huggingface.co/Nilaksh404/semantic-wm](https://huggingface.co/Nilaksh404/semantic-wm); project page lists NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations and Datasets Track — [project page](https://hskalin.github.io/semantic-wm/). **[Venue not checked on OpenReview]**

**Pixel-space counter-position**
- WMPO (Zhu et al., arXiv 2511.09515, Nov 2025) deliberately uses a pixel-space video world model (OpenSora-derived, with SDXL 2D VAE) because the VLA then sees generated images "consistent with its pretraining"; on MimicGen (Coffee, StackThree, ThreePieceAssembly, Square) with 128 rollouts WMPO 47.1% vs GRPO 33.2% vs DPO 37.3%; with 1280 rollouts 57.6% vs 37.1% vs 42.4%; real Cobot Mobile ALOHA square-on-stick insertion (5 mm clearance, 30 trials): base OpenVLA-OFT 53%, DPO 60%, WMPO 70% — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09515)
- Ctrl-World (Guo, Shi, Chen, Finn; arXiv 2510.10125): multi-view world model initialised from 1.5B Stable Video Diffusion (VAE latent video diffusion) trained on DROID (95k trajectories, 564 scenes); rankings of π0/π0-FAST/π0.5 in the model "closely correlated" with real-world rankings; filtered imagined successes used for SFT lifted π0.5-droid from 38.7% to 83.4% on novel instruction-following tasks on the real robot (+44.7 points) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10125)

### Inferences
- The field has converged on "predict in a pretrained or self-supervised feature space" for planning (DINO-WM, JEPA-WMs, V-JEPA 2-AC, RAE-NWM, Nilaksh et al.), while methods that train or fine-tune pixel-input VLAs in imagination (WMPO, Ctrl-World) keep a pixel or VAE-latent decoder because the policy consumes images. The prediction target is therefore tied to what the policy's encoder expects as input.
- Where the same architecture is held fixed and only the latent space changes (Nilaksh et al.; RAE-NWM's R² analysis; DINO-WM's decoder-coupling ablation), semantic/SSL features win on control-relevant metrics even when they lose on pixel fidelity. This is the most direct evidence for RQ1's "prediction target" factor, but it comes from offline/in-model evaluation.
- The V-JEPA 2-AC vs Cosmos comparison is the only real-robot, closed-loop head-to-head of a feature-space vs a generative video world model found, and it confounds prediction target with model size, training data and planning budget (16 s vs 4 min per action).

- Exact contents and quotes of LeCun (2022) were not retrieved (OpenReview blocked the fetch).
- V-JEPA 2.1 real-robot gain: +20 vs +10 points conflict unresolved; licence of V-JEPA 2.1 weights (MIT vs CC BY-NC-ND) unresolved.
- No numeric results extracted for DINO-world; no numbers for the 2026 JEPA follow-ups (Flow-JEPA etc.).

## Q2. Evidence on pretrained encoders for policies (R3M, VC-1, Theia, DINOv2, SigLIP, V-JEPA 2) and robustness to visual domain shift

### Takeaway
There is no universally best pretrained encoder for policies. In model-free imitation, emergent segmentation (DINO-like ViTs) and data diversity predict robustness better than ImageNet accuracy; in model-based RL (Dreamer, TD-MPC2) frozen PVRs often do not beat encoders trained from scratch in-distribution, but they help under severe visual shifts, especially with partial fine-tuning. Video-predictive encoders (V-JEPA 2) are an emerging option for VLAs.

### Cited Findings
- VC-1 / CortexBench (Majumdar et al., arXiv 2303.18240): 17 tasks (locomotion, navigation, dexterous and mobile manipulation); VC-1 is an MAE ViT trained on >4,000 h of egocentric video from seven sources (4.3M+ images) plus ImageNet; "scaling dataset size and diversity does not improve performance universally (but does so on average)"; no single PVR dominates; VC-1 best on average; >10,000 GPU-hours; code and models released — [arXiv 2303.18240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18240)
- Burns, Witzel, Hamid, Yu, Finn, Hausman, "What Makes Pre-Trained Visual Representations Successful for Robust Manipulation?" (arXiv 2312.12444): 15 pretrained models, ten tasks in two simulated environments with lighting, texture and distractor shifts; emergent segmentation score (ViT attention) predicts OOD generalisation better than ImageNet accuracy, in-domain accuracy or shape bias, and predicts real-world ALOHA performance after offline training with 50 demonstrations — [arXiv 2312.12444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12444). Venue CoRL 2024 **[UNVERIFIED]**
- Theia (Shang et al., CoRL 2024, arXiv 2407.20179): distils CLIP, Depth-Anything, DINOv2, SAM and ViT (best subset: CLIP+DINOv2+ViT) into Tiny/Small/Base models; CortexBench MuJoCo subset approx. Theia-B ~81 vs MVP-L ~77, R3M ~77, DINOv2-L ~73, CLIP-L ~71, VC-1-L ~70 (approximate values read from a figure summary); entropy of feature-norm distribution correlates with robot learning performance (R=0.943) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.20179); code/models at [theia.theaiinstitute.com](https://theia.theaiinstitute.com). **[Scores approximate; licence not confirmed]**
- Schneider, Krug, Vaskevicius, Palmieri, Boedecker (Bosch/Freiburg), "The Surprising Ineffectiveness of Pre-Trained Visual Representations for Model-Based Reinforcement Learning" (NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2411.10175): DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 with frozen CLIP, R3M, Taskonomy, VIP, DINOv2 ViT-G, OpenCLIP ViT-H, VC-1 ViT-L, R2D2 vs scratch/autoencoder, on DMC (5), ManiSkill2 (4), Miniworld (1). "Representations learned from scratch are in most cases equally or even more data-efficient than the PVRs"; scratch also beats PVRs OOD (VC-1 the partial exception); data diversity and ViT architecture matter most; language conditioning and sequential-data training do not; task performance correlates with reward-prediction accuracy (r = −0.66, p = 0.004); simulation only — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.10175v1); venue — [NeurIPS 2024 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/95560)
- Jones, Zhou, Pattinson, "Pre-trained Visual Representations Generalize Where it Matters in Model-Based Reinforcement Learning" (arXiv 2509.12531, Sept 2025): DreamerV3 with DINOv2 or CLIP replacing the CNN encoder; ManiSkill tabletop and RL-ViGen CARLA; under hard shifts (table texture; fog, rain, darkness) partially fine-tuned DINOv2 (last quarter of layers, LR 4e-6) drops 28% vs 106% for the scratch CNN; in CARLA frozen DINOv2 drops 6% and 33% (weak/strong shift) vs 79% and 87%; simulation only, 3 seeds — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12531)
- DINO-WM encoder ablation: DINOv2 patch features beat DINOv2 CLS, R3M and ResNet-18 as world-model latent spaces for planning; global (CLS) vectors fail on manipulation — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983)
- JEPA-VLA (Miao et al., Tsinghua, arXiv 2602.11832, Feb 2026): argues video-predictive embeddings (V-JEPA 2) discard unpredictable environment factors while keeping task-relevant dynamics, and adds them to VLAs; evaluated on LIBERO, LIBERO-plus, RoboTwin 2.0 and real-robot tasks — [arXiv 2602.11832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11832). **[Numbers not extracted]**
- OpenVLA fuses SigLIP and DINOv2 features in its vision backbone — [LearnOpenCV overview](https://learnopencv.com/vision-language-action-models-lerobot-policy/) (secondary source; the OpenVLA paper is the primary).

### Inferences
- For RQ1's "policy encoder" factor the literature predicts an interaction: frozen PVRs may not help (or may hurt) in-distribution inside a world model (Schneider et al.) but help under visual domain shift (Jones et al., Burns et al.), which is exactly the sim/imagination-to-real case. Partial fine-tuning looks like a strong default to test.
- Reward/success-relevant information is the bottleneck for PVR-based world models (Schneider r = −0.66; Nilaksh's success-classifier probe), so probing latents for task success and inverse dynamics is a cheap proxy metric worth including in the PhD protocol.

### Gaps
- No controlled real-robot study found that varies the policy encoder (DINOv2 vs SigLIP vs V-JEPA 2 vs R3M/VC-1/Theia) for a policy trained inside a world model and measures real-world success under visual shift.
- Not verified in this session: Hansen et al. 2022 ("On pre-training for visuo-motor control: revisiting a learning-from-scratch baseline") and Dasari et al. 2023 dataset studies, which would support the "scratch is competitive" point.
- Theia licence and exact CortexBench numbers not confirmed.

## Q3. Is there a controlled study of how the world model's prediction target affects the real-world performance of policies trained inside it? What is closest?

### Takeaway
I found no published study (to September 2026) that holds everything fixed, varies only the world model's prediction target (pixels vs VAE latents vs pretrained features), trains policies inside each model, and measures success on a real robot. The closest are Nilaksh et al. 2026 (controlled latent-space comparison on real BridgeData V2, but policies only evaluated inside the model, no training and no real deployment), V-JEPA 2-AC vs Cosmos (real robot, but MPC planning and uncontrolled confounds), and Ctrl-World/WMPO (real-robot gains from policies improved in pixel/VAE-latent world models, with no alternative latent spaces tested).

### Cited Findings
- Nilaksh et al. explicitly list as limitations: single Bridge V2 setting and embodiment, VLM-judged success, evaluation of a fixed policy "rather than policy improvement or sim-to-real transfer" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06388)
- V-JEPA 2-AC vs Cosmos on a real Franka (Lab 2): pick-and-place cup 80% vs 0%, 16 s vs 4 min per action; planning (MPC), no policy training — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985)
- DINO-WM shows that adding a pixel reconstruction loss to the feature predictor lowers planning success (0.92 to 0.80), simulation only — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983)
- WMPO real robot: 53% base to 70% after RL in a pixel-space world model; the authors argue pixel space is needed to match VLA pretraining, but do not ablate a feature-space model on the robot — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09515)
- Ctrl-World real robot: π0.5 38.7% to 83.4% after SFT on imagined successes from an SVD-based (VAE latent) world model — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10125)
- LeWM follow-up shows the representation regulariser changes downstream policy success (LIBERO 63.6% to 83.8%), a representation-to-policy link but in simulation — [arXiv 2607.26924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26924)
- Earlier reconstruction-free Dreamer variants: DreamingV2 (discrete latents + contrastive, no reconstruction) beats DreamerV2 by over 30% on 3D robot-arm tasks in simulation — [arXiv 2203.00494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00494); DayDreamer trains DreamerV2 (pixel reconstruction) directly on physical robots — [arXiv 2206.14176](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.14176). Search also surfaced Dreamer-CDP (reconstruction-free, [arXiv 2603.07083](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.07083)) **[not read]**
- A 2025 study of sim-to-real gaps in latent-space MBRL (Lin & Sun, arXiv 2506.12735) evaluates only in MuJoCo — [arXiv 2506.12735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12735)

### Inferences
- The gap is real and well-bounded: controlled comparisons exist offline (Nilaksh) and in simulation (DINO-WM, JEPA-WMs, LeWM, Schneider), and real-robot imagination training exists for a single latent space each (WMPO, Ctrl-World). A study crossing prediction target x policy encoder x amount/diversity of imagined data with real-robot evaluation would fill it directly. Nilaksh et al.'s released code and weights on BridgeData V2 (a WidowX setup reproducible in many labs) are a natural starting point.
- Expected tension to test: feature-space targets help planning and action-relevant structure, while pixel/VAE targets keep compatibility with image-input policies (WMPO's argument). A decoder from features (RAE-style, as in RAE-NWM) is one way to get both, and should be an arm of the experiment.

### Gaps
- Could not rule out unindexed workshop papers from mid/late 2026; the search covered arXiv and main venues only.
- No quantitative study found on how the amount or diversity of imagined data interacts with the prediction target for real-world policy success (Ctrl-World and WMPO report single settings; WMPO reports 128 vs 1280 rollouts only in simulation).
