## Discrepancies relative to content/06-state-of-the-art.md (visible text)

D1. Reference numbering in §6 does not match the visible list in §12. Examples: §6 cites DreamGen as [13], Cosmos Transfer via [12]/[4], DreamZero [14], DINO-WM [15], SkyJEPA [16], Ctrl-World [17], Veo [18], SIMPLER [19], co-training [23], mechanistic analysis [24], surgical fine-tuning [25], TwinRL [26], Xie [27]; §12 lists DreamGen [14], Cosmos-Transfer1 [13], DreamZero [15], DINO-WM [16], SkyJEPA [10], Ctrl-World [18], SIMPLER [19], Maddukuri [21], Lei [22], Kachaev [25], Lee [26], and has placeholders at [17] (ZANATTA_PLACEHOLDER) and [23] (IWS_PLACEHOLDER). Xie 2023/2024 and TwinRL do not appear in the visible §12 list at all.
D2. §6: "Cosmos Transfer ... turns simulator renderings such as depth or segmentation into photorealistic video with the same geometry and motion". The abstract says it generates "world simulations based on multiple spatial control inputs of various modalities such as segmentation, depth, and edge" for "Sim2Real"; "photorealistic" and "same geometry and motion" are a reasonable gloss but not abstract wording.
D3. §6: DreamGen "GR00T N1 policy learned 22 new behaviours from teleoperation data of a single task, and its success improved on three real robots". Abstract: "a humanoid robot can perform 22 new behaviors in both seen and unseen environments, while requiring teleoperation data from only a single pick-and-place task in one environment". The abstract does not name GR00T N1 and mentions one humanoid, not three robots. The "three real robots" claim needs a body-text citation or should be softened.
D4. §6: "SkyJEPA [16] trained a JEPA world model on simulated data and controlled a real quadrotor without real-world training." Abstract supports "zero-shot sim-to-real transfer" and outdoor closed-loop experiments, but says "automated dataset generation" rather than "simulated data", and "reduce the dependence on ... real-world data collection" rather than "without real-world training". Slightly overstated at abstract level; likely true in the paper body (not checked).
D5. §6 says the first WAM sim-to-real work [7] "reached 35% average success on a real Franka arm without any real training data" — matches the abstract ("no real demonstrations", "35% average success rate"). §12 [7] lists it as arXiv only; the arXiv comment adds "CVPR'26 Embodied AI Workshop" and says it is a preliminary result of a fuller release.
D6. §6 on Ctrl-World: "improved a policy by fine-tuning it on imagined successful trajectories" — abstract gives 44.7% improvement; fine. Venue ICLR 2026 poster (§12 [18] says ICLR 2026: correct). World-in-World (§12 [6]) is listed as 2026; arXiv v1 is 2025 and it is an ICLR 2026 oral.
D7. §6 says VLAW found world models "miss small contact details" and "are trained on demonstrations without failures" — matches the abstract. §12 [5] cites arXiv only; OpenReview shows ICML 2026 acceptance.
D8. §6: co-training "improved real-world manipulation [23]" — abstract: +38% average (RSS 2025). Mechanistic analysis: "aligns the two domains but keeps them distinguishable" matches "balance between cross-domain representation alignment and domain discernibility".
