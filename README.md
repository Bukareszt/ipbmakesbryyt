# IPB — Indywidualny Plan Badawczy (Individual Research Plan)

This repo is where we prepare the **Individual Research Plan (IPB / IRP)** required by the
Doctoral School of Wrocław University of Science and Technology (Szkoła Doktorska PWr).

- Official page: https://szd.pwr.edu.pl/doktoranci/indywidualny-plan-badawczy
- FAQ: https://szd.pwr.edu.pl/faq/indywidualny-plan-badawczy
- Mid-term evaluation (where the IPB is checked): https://szd.pwr.edu.pl/doktoranci/ocena-srodokresowa
- Upload system: https://raporty.szd.pwr.edu.pl/
- Official template: [`ipb.docx`](ipb.docx) (copy of the form from the SzD website)

The full section-by-section requirements are in **[SPEC.md](SPEC.md)**.

---

## 1. What the IPB is (and why it matters)

The IPB is the research plan for the whole doctorate. The doctoral student prepares it **in agreement
with the supervisor(s)**. Together with the study programme, it is the basis for the doctoral training.

It matters because:

| Why | Consequence |
|---|---|
| It must be submitted **within 12 months of starting the programme** | If it's late, the student **can be removed from the list of doctoral students** |
| It sets the **dissertation submission deadline** | If the dissertation isn't submitted by the IPB deadline, the student is removed (the deadline can be extended by up to 2 years under the School Regulations) |
| It is the reference document for the **mid-term evaluation** (start of semester 5) | The committee checks progress against *this* schedule. A negative result has serious consequences |
| From **semester 3** onward, individual study plans must follow the IPB schedule | Courses and activities should line up with the plan |

Legal basis: **§ 13 of the Doctoral School Regulations (Regulamin Szkoły Doktorskiej PWr)**. § 13 ust. 2
lists the required content, and § 13 ust. 3 covers changes. The Act (Ustawa PSWiN) comes in through
**art. 186 ust. 1 pkt 3** (the publication requirement for the degree) and **art. 267 ust. 2 pkt 2 lit. a/b**
(the ministerial lists of journals and publishers).

## 2. Target / deliverable

A **complete, signed, bilingual (PL/EN) IPB** in the official `ipb.docx` form, delivered as:

1. **A paper original** with signatures (doctoral student, supervisor(s), and the assistant supervisor's
   opinion and signature if one is appointed), handed in at the **Doctoral School Dean's office**.
2. **An electronic version** uploaded to **https://raporty.szd.pwr.edu.pl/**. For the mid-term evaluation
   the School asks for a signed scan plus an unsigned PDF, so it's safest to prepare both now.

Done = every one of the 15 form sections is filled, the page limits are respected, the dates are
consistent, and the plan is signed and submitted before the 12-month deadline.

## 3. Rules at a glance

- **Deadline:** 12 months from the start of the programme (in practice, the end of year 1). For a start on
  1 Oct 2025, that means by **30 Sep 2026**. Check your own start date.
- **Written with the supervisor.** If there's an assistant supervisor (promotor pomocniczy), they must
  give a written **opinion** (section 14) before you submit.
- **Language:** the form is bilingual. Headings are PL/EN, and the content can be written in one language
  (usually English). **Exception:** the popular-science abstract (section 10) must be in **both PL and EN,
  with identical content**.
- **Formatting of free-text sections:** **11 pt font, line spacing 1**. Page limits:

  | Section | Limit |
  |---|---|
  | 5. Justification of the topic | 1 page |
  | 6. State of the art | 2 pages |
  | 7. Research questions & hypotheses | 1 page |
  | 8. Contribution to the discipline | 1 page |
  | 9. Research methods | 2 pages |
  | 10. Popular-science abstract | 1 page (per language) |
  | 12. Other | 1 page |

- **Dissertation submission date (section 4):** must be **no later than the end of semester 8**. The
  recommended value is the last day of the 4-year period (start 1.10.2025 → **30.09.2029**).
- **Publication date (section 11):** the date a paper goes to review or print. It must be **at least one
  article** in a journal or peer-reviewed international conference proceedings on the **ministerial list**
  in the year of publication, **or one monograph** from a listed publisher (art. 186 ust. 1 pkt 3). This
  is a degree requirement, so plan it realistically. Semesters 3–6 are typical.
- **Topic (section 2):** a proposal. It **doesn't have to be the final dissertation title**.
- **Changes:** the IPB can be **updated only after the mid-term evaluation**, in justified cases, in
  agreement with the supervisor. The **Dean decides** after consulting the discipline director. So plan
  well, because you effectively get one revision.

## 4. Know-how (how to write a good IPB)

The mid-term committee (3 members of your discipline, one of them external, and **not** your supervisor)
will compare your autoreferat with **this document**. Write it so that you can be evaluated fairly
against it.

1. **Make the schedule checkable.** For each semester, list concrete, verifiable outputs such as "submit
   paper X to venue Y", "build test stand", "dataset collected". Avoid vague items like "continue research".
   Semesters 1–4 matter most, because they're what the mid-term committee reviews.
2. **Use the template's activity types.** Include research execution (setup, materials,
   measurements/experiments, analysis), article submission, patent application, industry
   implementation proposal, conferences/workshops/summer schools, grant applications (e.g. NCN Preludium),
   and domestic/foreign mobility (visits, consultations). Also say **where** the research happens (lab,
   partner institution).
3. **Keep the chain consistent:** hypotheses (7) → methods (9) → schedule tasks (3) → expected
   contribution (8). Each hypothesis should have a method and a place in the schedule.
4. **The publication date (11) must match the schedule (3).** If section 11 says "October 2026", then some
   semester in section 3 must include "prepare and submit article".
5. **Be conservative and realistic.** Missing IPB milestones is the main risk at mid-term. It's better to
   under-promise in the plan and over-deliver in the autoreferat.
6. **State of the art (6):** cite the key works (around 15–30 references is typical for 2 pages) and end by
   naming the **research gap** the thesis fills. That gap is what justifies sections 5 and 8.
7. **Popular-science abstract (10):** no jargon. Cover the goal, what you'll do, why it matters, and the
   expected results. Write it once, translate it, and check that the PL and EN versions say the same thing.
8. **Cooperation with the supervisor (13):** be specific, e.g. "weekly 1h meeting (in person/Teams),
   monthly written progress summary, communication via e-mail/Slack, shared repo/Overleaf".
9. **Metadata (1):** full name, discipline, faculty, department, start date, **ORCID** (create one at
   orcid.org if needed), and supervisors with their **affiliation if they're external to PWr**.
10. **Signatures:** collect them early. Supervisors travel, and the deadline is fixed.

## 5. Workflow

```
[1] Collect metadata & agree topic with supervisor
[2] Draft content sections in Markdown (content/*.md)      ← versioned in this repo
[3] Internal consistency check (see SPEC.md §4 checklist)
[4] Supervisor review → revise
[5] Assistant supervisor opinion (if applicable)
[6] Transfer text into ipb.docx, check page limits at 11 pt / spacing 1
[7] Print, sign (student + supervisor(s) + assistant supervisor)
[8] Hand in paper original at the Dean's office + upload to raporty.szd.pwr.edu.pl
[9] Keep a copy: it is the baseline for the mid-term autoreferat
```
