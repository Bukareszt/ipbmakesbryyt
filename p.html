GAIA-2: Pushing the Boundaries of Video Generative Models for Safer Assisted and Automated Driving

Training autonomous driving systems to handle the unexpected is one of the biggest challenges in the industry. With GAIA-2, we’re unlocking a new frontier in video generation—empowering developers to create richly diverse, controllable, and safety-critical driving scenarios at scale.

Technical Report

Training and validating AI for assisted and automated driving requires exposure to diverse, complex, and safety-critical scenarios. However, relying solely on real-world data collection is limited by cost and time. Rare but critical situations such as sudden cut-ins, unpredictable pedestrian behavior, or extreme weather conditions are difficult to capture in sufficient quantities. 

GAIA-2 changes this. We’re excited to introduce our cutting-edge video-generative world model tailored for global autonomous driving. Building on the foundation of GAIA-1, GAIA-2 pushes the boundaries of synthetic data generation with enhanced controllability, expanded geographic diversity, and broader vehicle representation. Unlike general-purpose generative models, GAIA-2 is purpose-built to navigate the complexities of driving—handling multiple camera viewpoints, diverse road conditions, and critical corner cases. By offering fine-grained control over key driving factors, GAIA-2 empowers engineers and researchers to create richer, more realistic training scenarios, accelerating the path to safer and more robust autonomy.

Overview: Synthetic Scenario Generation for Real-world Safety

Generative world models are emerging as a powerful tool in advancing assisted and automated driving systems, offering scalable, diverse synthetic data for training and evaluation. Unlike general-purpose text-to-video and image-to-video models, domain-specific generative frameworks must provide fine-grained control over the driving dynamics, spanning ego-vehicle behavior, interactions with other road users, and environmental factors such as road topology, weather, and time of day. Spatiotemporal consistency across multiple camera viewpoints is equally critical to ensure accurate perception and reasoning. Just as important is the ability to synthesize rare but safety-critical scenarios such as ambiguous intersections or unpredictable pedestrian behavior, which is essential for building more robust and reliable AI driving systems.

GAIA-2 is designed to meet these challenges. It combines a latent diffusion architecture with extensive domain-specific conditioning to enable precise control over multi-camera video generation. By expanding its geographic coverage to the UK, US, and Germany, GAIA-2 enhances realism and adaptability across varied driving conditions. It supports long-horizon, stable video synthesis with flexible operational modes from generating entirely new driving scenes to seamlessly inserting dynamic agents into existing footage. Its conditioning parameters span key driving factors, which include ego-action variables (e.g., speed, steering curvature), environmental factors (weather, time of day), and road configurations (e.g., number of drivable lanes, speed limits, pedestrian crossings, intersections). This level of control allows GAIA-2 to generate both typical scenarios and high-risk edge cases, making it a powerful and unified framework for training, testing, and validating autonomous AI driving models.

Advancing Safety and Validation through Synthetic Scenarios

Diverse Video Generation

GAIA-2 unlocks a new level of scenario diversity by dynamically adapting to varied geographic, temporal, and environmental conditions—a critical requirement for robust validation. GAIA-2 can synthesize driving scenes across multiple countries, times of day, weather conditions, and road types. For instance, it can recreate left-hand traffic in the UK, distinct road signage in the US, and European lane markings in Germany.

Beyond geographic variability, GAIA-2 enables effortless modulation of time of day and weather conditions, allowing scenarios to seamlessly transition between dawn, midday, and night, or clear, rainy, and foggy conditions. It also supports smooth adaptation across urban, suburban, and highway environments.  

By generating realistic, diverse, and precisely controlled scenarios, GAIA-2 reduces reliance on extensive, location-specific real-world data collection. This accelerates testing and validation workflows and helps AI driving systems generalize across regions, adapt to domain shifts effectively, and perform reliably in both routine and edge-case scenarios.

Video Below: Examples of the diversity of the scene generated by GAIA-2. Multiple geographies (UK, US, Germany), in different lighting and weather conditions.

Augmenting Real-World Scenarios

On-road testing captures a vast spectrum of real-world driving conditions, from common traffic patterns to rare safety-critical situations. However, each recorded log offers only a single realization of an event: one combination of environmental conditions, agent behaviors, and outcomes dictated by a specific set of actions. These logs typically pair video footage with labeled driving actions, indicating whether a maneuver was appropriate in that given context. While valuable, this one-shot perspective fails to capture the full range of possible variations that could unfold under different conditions, such as changes in weather, times of day, or nearby road users. 

This limitation underscores a fundamental challenge: real-world data alone is insufficient for comprehensive validation. Every recorded scenario represents just one possible outcome,  leaving gaps in coverage for edge cases and rare events. GAIA-2 bridges this gap by augmenting and extending real-world scenarios, exploring how situations might unfold under different conditions. This enhances test coverage and model robustness, ensuring AI driving systems are grounded in reality while being prepared for the full complexity of real-world driving.

Video Below: A real-world scenario augmented by adjusting the weather and lighting conditions while keeping all the other key driving features unchanged

Video in Tabs: The top row shows an actual real-world scenario, while the bottom rows display several generated versions of that same scene.

Generating Observations for a Given Action

GAIA-2 introduces a powerful capability: synthesizing entire driving scenes conditioned on a target action. By specifying an action—such as braking, yielding, or performing a U-turn—GAIA-2 generates diverse, contextually valid video sequences where that action remains both necessary and appropriate. 

Crucially, these generated scenarios are not repetitions of a fixed template; instead, they vary across road layouts, traffic densities, weather conditions, and lighting, to test behavior under a broad set of conditions. This action-conditioned generation enables systematic, scalable exploration of scenario space, significantly improving validation. By exposing AI driving systems to a rich spectrum of action-driven observations, GAIA-2 supports more robust, safer, and consistent behavior across real-world-like driving conditions, even in rare or high-risk situations.

Video in Tabs: Scenarios built around specific ego-actions. For any given action, the resulting videos capture the full range of possible contexts where that action might occur.

Tackling the Long Tail of Safety-Critical Scenarios

Safety-critical events, such as near-collisions, sudden cut-ins, and emergency braking, are among the most challenging yet essential scenarios to capture in real-world data. Their rarity in real-world driving logs makes it difficult to systematically train and validate systems for high-stakes decision-making. GAIA-2 addresses this by enabling precise, controlled generation of high-risk scenarios, where every agent’s position, motion, and interaction can be explicitly defined. It can proactively simulate pre-collision situations, emergency maneuvers (e.g., harsh braking), and even out-of-distribution behavior (e.g., drifting or sudden obstructions). 

This repeatable, controlled scenario synthesis expands test coverage with critical corner cases that are underrepresented in real-world datasets. By exposing systems to these safety-critical conditions in a controlled setting, GAIA-2 enables rigorous validation of fail-safe behaviors and builds resilience long before deployment on public roads.

Videos in Tabs: These safety-critical scenarios highlight our capability to influence other agents in order to produce hazardous conditions.

Into the Unknown: GAIA-2’s Out-of-Distribution Capability

While safety-critical scenarios are paramount for evaluating autonomous systems, the ability to generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions—such as unseen cities, road types, camera configurations, or traffic patterns—is equally important for real-world deployment.

GAIA-2 is trained on a diverse dataset with significant variability across geographies, vehicle platforms, and environmental factors. This allows it to synthesise surprising, unusual, or entirely novel conditions, offering insights into its robustness, generalization, and creative flexibility. By synthesizing these OOD scenarios, GAIA-2 provides a valuable tool for testing the robustness of AV models under domain shifts, supporting more generalizable and resilient driving systems.

Videos in Tabs: Examples of generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios. By conditioning GAIA-2 on ego-actions, we can generate states that lie entirely outside the training distribution, extending well beyond the scenarios encountered during training.

Practical Applications and Benefits

GAIA-2’s ability to generate diverse, high-fidelity driving scenarios provides tangible benefits for assisted and automated driving development. By synthesizing both common driving conditions and elusive corner cases—such as unpredictable agents, extreme weather, or unusual traffic patterns —it significantly expands test coverage, ensuring AI driving models are exposed to a far richer set of training and evaluation scenarios than traditional data collection allows.

This accelerates iteration cycles by enabling large-scale, repeatable testing without the costs and risks of on-road data. Developers can systematically probe and address model weaknesses, leading to faster refinement and more resilient performance in real-world environments. By bridging the gap between simulation and deployment, GAIA-2 plays a key role in improving the safety, reliability, and readiness of AI driving systems.

Model Architecture and Training

GAIA-2 is a highly controllable video-generative world model, designed to simulate realistic and diverse driving environments with fine-grained control over ego-vehicle actions, dynamic agents, and scene features. At its core, it comprises two key components: a video tokenizer and a latent diffusion world model, which work together to generate high-fidelity simulations.

The video tokenizer compresses raw pixel-space videos into a compact, semantically meaningful latent space, enabling efficient representation of driving dynamics while preserving critical details. The latent diffusion world model then predicts future latent states based on past observations, ego-vehicle actions (e.g., speed, steering curvature), dynamic agent behaviors (based on 3D bounding boxes), environmental factors (weather, time of day), and road attributes (e.g., number of lanes, speed limits, cycle and bus lanes, zebra crossings, intersections, traffic lights). 

GAIA-2 also supports latent conditioning from external models, including CLIP embeddings and a proprietary model optimised for driving, enhancing its adaptability for diverse synthetic data applications. This architecture allows GAIA-2 to operate in multiple generative modes: it can forecast future frames, synthesise entirely new scenes, or modify existing video sequences. By leveraging structured conditioning, GAIA-2 ensures spatiotemporal consistency across multiple camera views, making it a powerful tool for diverse, scalable, realistic, and corner-case-rich AV scenario generation.

GAIA-2 was trained on a large-scale, curated dataset of video sequences, covering diverse geographic, environmental, and driving conditions across the UK, US, and Germany. Data collection spanned multiple vehicle platforms, sensor configurations, and frame rates, ensuring broad realism and adaptability. To improve model generalization, we applied balanced sampling strategies and held out specific geographic regions for validation.

For a deeper dive into GAIA-2’s architecture, training methodology, and evaluation, please refer to our technical report.

Technical Report

Advancing from GAIA-1 to GAIA-2: Greater Control, Realism, and Scalability

GAIA-2 introduces major advancements over GAIA-1, addressing the increasing need for controllability, realism, and efficiency in synthetic data generation.

Expanded Control and Realism: One of the key differentiators of GAIA-2 is its broader scope of control, allowing for fine-grained scenario customisation. While GAIA-1 had limited conditioning capabilities, GAIA-2 supports multiple geographies (UK, US, Germany), enabling region-specific data generation that accelerates the adaptation of AI driving models to new driving domains. It also introduces native multi-camera video generation, ensuring spatial and temporal coherence across different viewpoints—an essential capability for learning robust driving policies from multi-view data. GAIA-2 further expands a more comprehensive set of domain-specific parameters, allowing researchers to modify weather conditions, road attributes, traffic dynamics, and agent behaviors with greater precision. This enhanced controllability makes GAIA-2 a more powerful tool for targeted scenario generation, whether for training driving models, augmenting real-world datasets, or stress-testing AI driving systems against rare but safety-critical edge cases.

Architectural Advancements: GAIA-2 also introduces a more streamlined video generation pipeline, improving scene consistency and visual fidelity. Unlike GAIA-1, which relied on frame-by-frame token generation via an autoregressive transformer, GAIA-2 employs a video tokenizer with a latent diffusion model, encoding entire sequences into a continuous latent space. This shift eliminates temporal discontinuities, enhances motion smoothness, and ensures multi-camera spatial coherence. The result is a more stable, scalable, and realistic generative framework.

Together, these enhancements position GAIA-2 as a new benchmark in generative world modeling for autonomy, delivering richer, more realistic, and more controllable synthetic driving scenarios.

Video below: A side-by-side comparison of GAIA-1 and GAIA-2’s video-generative capability.

Conclusion: Paving the Way for Safer, Scalable Embodied AI Development

GAIA-2 represents a major step forward in generative world modeling for assisted and automated driving. It combines unparalleled controllability, geographic diversity, and high-fidelity multi-camera video synthesis to systematically generate diverse, edge-case-rich scenarios. By expanding test coverage and accelerating model iteration, GAIA-2 strengthens the robustness of AI driving systems.

As the need for scalable, safety-critical validation grows, generative models like GAIA-2 will play a crucial role in closing the gap between simulation and real-world deployment. Moving forward, we are excited to explore further advancements in controllability, scene realism, and agent interaction modeling—unlocking new possibilities for the future of self-driving AI.

Future Work and Challenges

While GAIA-2 sets a new benchmark, key areas for continued improvement are:

  • Minimizing artifacts and hallucinations – As with all generative models, occasional inconsistencies may emerge, especially in complex, long-horizon scenes. Developing better failure detection mechanisms and refining generation constraints will be critical in maintaining high-quality, reliable outputs.
  • Improving efficient generation at scale – Although GAIA-2 supports parallel generation, generation speed remains an important consideration for real-world deployment. Future work will explore techniques such as distillation and model parallelism at inference to further accelerate video synthesis without compromising quality.
  • Continued expansion of scenario diversity – With GAIA-2 already supporting multiple geographies and environmental conditions, further enriching the dataset with rare and high-risk edge cases will continue to strengthen validation.

These areas of exploration represent ongoing efforts to push the boundaries of generative modeling, ensuring that GAIA-2 and its successors become even more powerful tools in the autonomous driving pipeline.
